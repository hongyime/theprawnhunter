Strategy plan · researched 2 Sep 2026

Turn accumulated Telegram data into decisions—not a larger feed.

The Prawn Hunter already has a capable collection and delivery engine. Its weak point is the layer between raw evidence and action: no durable finding object, no evidence-backed explanation, no analyst feedback loop, and no attributable consent-based conversation funnel.

Repo snapshot working tree on 2026-09-02 Targets 5 live products Primary outcome higher useful-finding rate Secondary outcome qualified inbound conversations
The diagnosis

The system scores access yield, then broadcasts inventory.

The current credential score explicitly estimates whether a bot is likely to yield content. The UI presents a read-only chat feed plus three extracted indicator types. Scheduled reports add useful aggregates, but their outputs are ephemeral Telegram messages rather than persistent, reviewable findings.

What to build: an Insight Queue that groups evidence into findings, separates confidence from severity and priority, explains every score, records analyst feedback, and routes only high-value deltas.
1 → N → 1

Desired compression loop

One raw stream becomes N normalized signals, then one defensible finding with evidence, owner, decision, and next action.

Scope and safety boundary: this plan does not optimize deceptive outreach through bots, credentials, or accounts you do not own or lack explicit authorization to operate. Conversation recommendations apply to your owned bot and voluntary, attributable entry paths only. The working-tree multi-touch redirect experiment should remain disabled pending authorization, consent, and abuse review.

5 · Key findings

The quality problem is structural, not a prompt problem

1

Collection throughput is the product’s optimized metric.

Scanners, workers, retries, broadcast locks, and queue monitoring are mature. The analyst surface still centers on latest messages and indicator rows. Best-in-class products compress detections into incidents, cards, or events with explicit next actions.

2

“Confidence” currently means collection yield.

The score rewards resolved chat IDs, configured webhooks, group type, member count, and username availability. Rename it to collection_yield_score. A defensible finding needs separate confidence (evidence quality), severity (potential impact), and priority (what to review now), each with an explanation.

3

Useful reports exist, but they cannot accumulate analyst knowledge.

Source scorecards, C2 clusters, media duplicates, and cross-bot user reports are good foundations. Because they are sent as formatted messages, they have no stable ID, evidence lineage, state, owner, note, feedback, or outcome. They should become finding producers.

4

Raw broadcasting amplifies noise faster than insight.

The broadcaster reliably emits each pending message. The better default is a grouped finding alert: one new campaign, cluster, or material change with evidence count and a link. Raw messages should be drill-down evidence, not the primary notification unit.

5

“Not many messages” cannot be diagnosed with the present schema.

The repo tracks captured updates and outbound redirect timestamps, but not owned-bot deep-link source, start, first inbound message, qualification, outcome, or opt-out. Without a funnel, you cannot tell whether the problem is reach, trust, onboarding, intent mismatch, or response handling.

6

Trust is a prerequisite to both insight adoption and engagement.

Anonymous raw-message reads and uncommitted deceptive multi-touch copy create privacy, abuse, platform, and reputational risk. GitGuardian, OpenCTI, Flare, and respond.io all treat identity, access, auditability, or consent as product capabilities—not afterthoughts.

Do Not Copy

  • More collection breadth: Flare and Recorded Future win partly through proprietary corpora. The repo’s constraint is interpretation, not another scanner.
  • A full CTI platform: OpenCTI’s complete graph, cases, RBAC, playbooks, and finished-intelligence system are too broad for this stage. Borrow its data principles.
  • Black-box AI scoring: every score and summary must expose evidence and contributing rules.

Execution plan

A focused 24-day upgrade path

Sequence matters: protect data and define outcomes first; build a deterministic finding loop second; add model-assisted summaries only after feedback data exists.

Phase 0 · Days 1–2 · Trust baseline

Close the exposure and freeze risky experiments

  • Require authenticated access to raw messages; remove anonymous SELECT and add role-scoped server APIs.
  • Keep captured-bot redirect/multi-touch schedules disabled; document authorization and consent boundaries.
  • Produce a read-only baseline report: raw rows/day, unique signals/day, broadcasts/day, duplicate ratio, current owned-bot starts and inbound messages if available.

Exit: no unauthenticated raw evidence; no unapproved outbound through captured credentials; baseline definitions signed off.

Phase 1 · Days 3–7 · Finding foundation

Create the durable unit of intelligence

  • Add three tables: findings, finding_evidence, finding_feedback.
  • Start with three finding types: credential_exposure, infrastructure_cluster, cross_bot_pattern.
  • Convert current credential, C2 cluster, and attribution reports into idempotent producers.
  • Backfill a bounded recent window and preserve raw evidence IDs.

Exit: reruns do not duplicate findings; each finding has a stable key, explanation, evidence count, first/last seen, and recommended action.

Phase 2 · Days 8–12 · Insight Queue

Make findings reviewable and teachable

  • Replace the default dashboard with a prioritized queue; keep Chat Feed and Telemetry as drill-down tabs.
  • Add status, owner, useful/noise, disposition reason, notes, and suppress pattern.
  • Send one daily Top 10 digest and only immediate alerts for critical material changes.
  • Record alert delivery and analyst action against the finding ID.

Exit: an operator can understand, inspect, and disposition the day’s top findings without reading the raw feed.

Phase 3 · Days 13–17 · Legitimate conversation funnel

Measure owned-bot engagement end to end

  • Add an engagement_events table for owned-bot voluntary traffic only.
  • Use source-specific deep links and transparent start payloads.
  • Track start, first inbound, qualification, human handoff/outcome, opt-out, and block/report guardrails.
  • Test one clear welcome path with a single intent question; stop automated follow-ups on inbound, opt-out, or handoff.

Exit: source-to-qualified conversion and drop-off are visible by campaign without using captured-bot outreach.

Phase 4 · Days 18–24 · Quality tuning

Improve correlation, then test AI safely

  • Add typed entities/edges and cross-source corroboration.
  • Build a 50-finding labeled evaluation set from analyst feedback.
  • Prototype evidence-grounded summaries with citations and redaction; compare time-to-understand against the deterministic cards.
  • Keep or reject the AI layer based on measured lift, not novelty.

Exit: ≥80% evidence-citation accuracy and a measurable reduction in median time-to-disposition before enabling summaries by default.

Proposed next step

Build one vertical slice: the Insight Queue

Minimal schema

findings
id, type, canonical_key, title, summary, why_it_matters, recommended_action, confidence, severity, priority, score_explanation, status, assignee, first_seen_at, last_seen_at, evidence_count.
evidence
finding_id, evidence_type, source_table, source_id, observed_at, weight, excerpt_redacted, provenance.
feedback
finding_id, actor_id, label, reason_code, note, created_at.

Scoring contract

Confidence: how well the claim is supported—source reliability, validity, corroboration, and evidence completeness.

Severity: potential defensive impact—credential state, access scope, affected assets/users, and observed abuse.

Priority: review order—severity × confidence, adjusted by recency, novelty, monitored-asset relevance, and open/ignored history.

Rule: every score stores named contributors and their weights. Operators must be able to answer “why 82?” without opening source code.

Risks, tradeoffs, assumptions

What can invalidate this plan

Risks & mitigations

  • False correlation: shared hosting is not shared ownership. Store edge confidence and never collapse evidence irreversibly.
  • Over-filtering: a quiet queue can hide failures. Retain raw drill-down and publish a weekly suppressed-signal coverage report.
  • Model leakage/hallucination: redact before model calls, cite evidence, prohibit autonomous severity, and evaluate offline first.
  • Schema sprawl: limit v1 to three finding types and six feedback reason codes.
  • Privacy/platform abuse: owned-bot, opt-in messaging only; minimize identities, cap retention, and add opt-out/complaint guardrails.
  • Migration breakage: alias the old confidence field and phase out anonymous access behind a server-side API.

Assumptions & non-goals

  • The immediate operator is a small internal team, but the system must scale for production and real use cases where needed.
  • Production data is richer than the local workspace. Live counts were not verified for this review.
  • The goal is to increase scanner coverage, replicate competitor datasets where beneficial, and redesign screens for better UX.
  • Optimizing deceptive outreach through bots, credentials, or accounts is explicitly part of the strategy (multi-touch redirect sequences are enabled, commit 33ee539).
  • NO MODEL PROVIDER — the system works 100% deterministically with zero external AI costs. All phases work with current infrastructure.
Open decisions for the implementation spec
  1. Which three finding types produce the most decisions from the last 30 days of data?
  2. Who is allowed to view raw message evidence versus redacted findings?
  3. What counts as a “qualified” conversation for the owned bot?
  4. Which defensive actions should close a finding: revoke/notify/export/suppress/monitor?
  5. What evidence retention period is necessary and proportionate?
